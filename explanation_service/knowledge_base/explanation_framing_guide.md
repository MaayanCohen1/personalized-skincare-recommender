# Explanation Framing Guide

A strong skincare explanation should connect the user’s skin condition with the product’s role in the routine and then support that connection with one or two relevant formulation or ingredient facts.

The first sentence of an explanation should usually answer why this specific product fits the user’s skin condition. The second sentence should usually support that fit with one or two relevant ingredient or formulation details.

For dry skin, explanations should emphasize hydration, moisture retention, barrier support, softness, and comfort. They should avoid focusing on mattifying, oil-control, or deep-cleansing language unless the product clearly requires it.

For oily skin, explanations should emphasize lightweight hydration, balanced feel, oil-aware support, and non-heavy comfort. They should avoid sounding like the user needs a rich or heavy dry-skin cream.

For oily and acne-prone skin, explanations should emphasize gentle cleansing, pore-aware support, light hydration, and comfort without heaviness. They should avoid overly aggressive or stripping wording.

For sensitive skin, explanations should emphasize soothing support, barrier comfort, and lower-irritation fit when supported by the product data. They should avoid strong or overstated language.

For dry and sensitive skin, explanations should emphasize both comfort and barrier support. They should sound calm, gentle, and supportive rather than active-heavy or oil-control focused.

For combination skin, explanations should emphasize balance, non-heavy hydration, and a flexible skin feel. They should avoid sounding too rich or too drying.

For normal skin, explanations should emphasize maintaining balance, comfort, and daily skin support rather than correcting an extreme problem.

When the product is a cleanser, explanations should focus on how it cleanses and how it leaves the skin feeling. When the product is a moisturizer, explanations should focus on hydration and barrier comfort. When the product is an SPF, explanations should combine daily protection with skin feel. When the product is a serum, explanations should focus on its targeted supporting role.

When the product is optional in the routine, the explanation should sound supportive rather than essential. Optional steps should be framed as helpful additions, not as the main solution.

If the product metadata suggests soothing, barrier support, hydration, lightweight feel, or oil control, explanations should use those formulation-level ideas before falling back to generic ingredient summaries.

Explanations should not read like ingredient dictionaries. They should read like a brief reasoned match between the user’s skin needs and the selected product.

Explanations should avoid repeating the same exact structure across all products. The wording should vary depending on the product category, the product benefits, and the user’s skin condition.
