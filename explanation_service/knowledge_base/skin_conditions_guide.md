# Skin Conditions Guide

Dry skin often feels tight and may look flaky, rough, dull, or slightly red. It usually benefits from hydration support, moisture retention, and barrier-focused care that helps reduce discomfort and improve skin comfort.

Dry skin is often linked to reduced moisture retention and weaker barrier function. Product explanations for dry skin should emphasize hydration, soothing support, and help for maintaining softness and comfort.

Oily skin is usually associated with visible shine, excess sebum, and sometimes enlarged pores. It often benefits from lightweight hydration, balanced cleansing, and formulas that avoid a heavy or greasy feel.

Oily skin can still be dehydrated. In these cases, the skin may show excess oil while still needing water-binding and barrier-supportive ingredients, so explanations should not assume that oily skin never needs hydration.

Combination skin usually means the T-zone is oilier while other areas, such as the cheeks, feel normal or drier. Product explanations for combination skin should emphasize balance, non-heavy hydration, and flexible support across different areas of the face.

Sensitive skin is more reactive and may be associated with redness, stinging, burning, irritation, or product intolerance. Explanations for sensitive skin should emphasize soothing support, low-irritation fit, and barrier comfort.

Normal skin is relatively balanced, without strong oiliness, dryness, or frequent irritation. Product explanations for normal skin should focus on maintaining comfort, supporting the skin barrier, and preserving overall balance.

Acne-prone skin is often associated with clogged pores, visible blemishes, and recurring breakouts. Explanations for acne-prone skin should emphasize gentle cleansing, pore-aware support, and lightweight hydration rather than harsh stripping.

Dry and sensitive skin often overlap. In this case, explanations should emphasize both barrier support and soothing hydration, while avoiding strong oil-control or mattifying language.

Oily and acne-prone skin often overlap. In this case, explanations should emphasize lightweight hydration, gentle pore care, and non-heavy textures rather than rich, occlusive wording.

Acne can also appear in skin that is dry, sensitive, or irritated. In these cases, explanations should avoid overly aggressive language and should emphasize gentle support, comfort, and barrier-aware care.

Skin condition can change over time due to weather, hormones, stress, age, routine changes, and overuse of harsh products. Product explanations should stay focused on the current visible or reported condition rather than assuming a fixed lifelong skin type.
