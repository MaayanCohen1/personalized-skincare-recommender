# Skin Conditions Guide

Oily skin is characterized by excess sebum production, particularly in the T-zone (forehead, nose, and chin). Dermatologists suggest that individuals with oily skin may benefit from lightweight, non-comedogenic moisturizers and oil-free formulations. Ingredients like niacinamide and salicylic acid are commonly recommended for oily skin routines because they help regulate surface oil without stripping the skin's natural moisture barrier.

Dry skin often presents with tightness, flaking, and a rough texture due to insufficient lipid production or impaired barrier function. The American Academy of Dermatology recommends using emollient-rich products containing ingredients such as hyaluronic acid, glycerin, and ceramides to help restore hydration. Avoiding harsh surfactants and opting for gentle, fragrance-free cleansers can also support the skin's barrier repair process.

Sensitive skin is prone to redness, stinging, and irritation in response to environmental factors or certain cosmetic ingredients. The European Academy of Dermatology and Venereology advises choosing products with minimal ingredient lists, free from common irritants such as artificial fragrances and alcohol. Soothing agents like aloe vera and zinc oxide-based sunscreens are often well-tolerated by individuals with sensitive skin.
