# Ingredients Dictionary

Niacinamide, also known as vitamin B3, is a water-soluble vitamin widely incorporated into skincare formulations. Research published in the Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology indicates that topical niacinamide supports the skin's natural barrier function and may help maintain an even skin tone by influencing melanin transfer between cells. It is generally well-tolerated across different skin types.

Hyaluronic acid is a naturally occurring glycosaminoglycan found in the skin's extracellular matrix. According to reviews in the International Journal of Biological Macromolecules, it functions as a humectant capable of binding up to 1000 times its weight in water, thereby helping the skin retain moisture and maintain a plump, hydrated appearance. It is commonly used in serums and moisturizers for all skin types.

Salicylic acid is a beta-hydroxy acid (BHA) derived from willow bark. The American Academy of Dermatology notes that it is oil-soluble, allowing it to penetrate into pores and help exfoliate dead skin cells from within the follicle. It is frequently included in formulations designed for oily and blemish-prone skin to help keep pores clear.

Retinol is a derivative of vitamin A that belongs to the retinoid family. According to the Cosmetic Ingredient Review (CIR), retinol supports the skin's natural cell turnover process and has been associated with improvements in the appearance of fine lines and uneven texture over time. Users are advised to introduce retinol gradually, as it may increase the skin's sensitivity to UV exposure.

Vitamin C, primarily in its L-ascorbic acid form, is a potent antioxidant used in skincare. Studies published in the Journal of Clinical and Aesthetic Dermatology report that it helps neutralize free radicals generated by UV radiation and environmental pollutants, supporting a brighter and more even complexion. It is most effective at acidic pH levels and is often formulated in concentrations between 10 and 20 percent.

Glycerin is one of the most widely used humectants in cosmetic formulations. Data from the Cosmetic Ingredient Review confirms that glycerin draws moisture from the environment to the skin surface and helps strengthen the skin's natural moisture barrier. It is suitable for nearly all skin types, including sensitive skin.

Zinc oxide is an inorganic mineral compound used in physical sunscreens. According to the FDA monograph on over-the-counter sunscreen active ingredients, zinc oxide provides broad-spectrum protection against both UVA and UVB radiation by sitting on the skin surface and reflecting or scattering UV photons. It is generally considered gentle and is often recommended for sensitive skin.

Aloe vera gel is extracted from the leaves of the Aloe barbadensis plant and has been used in skincare for centuries. The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) notes that it contains polysaccharides and glycoproteins that contribute to its moisturizing and soothing properties. It is commonly applied to help calm irritated or sun-exposed skin.
