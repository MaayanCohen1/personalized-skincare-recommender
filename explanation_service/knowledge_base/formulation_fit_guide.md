# Formulation Fit Guide

For dry skin, richer and more cushioning textures are often a better fit than very light, fast-drying, or strongly mattifying formulas. Explanations should highlight comfort, moisture retention, and support for a dry-feeling skin barrier.

For dry skin, humectants help attract water, emollients help soften the skin, and barrier-supportive ingredients help reduce moisture loss. Product explanations can refer to these functions when connecting a formula to dryness support.

For oily skin, lightweight hydration is often a better fit than heavy or greasy textures. Explanations should highlight non-heavy moisture, balanced feel, and support that does not leave the skin feeling overloaded.

For oily skin, harsh cleansing and over-stripping are not ideal. Explanations should avoid suggesting that stronger cleansing is always better, and should instead emphasize balanced cleansing and light support.

For combination skin, products with a balanced skin feel are often a better fit than formulas that are extremely rich or extremely drying. Explanations should reflect flexibility, light hydration, and skin balance.

For sensitive skin, formulas that are gentle, low-irritation, and supportive of skin comfort are often preferred. Explanations should highlight soothing support, barrier comfort, and reduced irritation potential when the product data supports that.

For dry and sensitive skin, richer hydration and barrier support may be especially relevant. Explanations should emphasize comfort, moisture retention, and gentle support rather than oil control or mattifying effects.

For oily and acne-prone skin, lighter textures such as gel-creams, light lotions, and non-heavy formulas are often easier to tolerate. Explanations should emphasize lightweight hydration, comfort, and pore-aware support.

When a product is fragrance-free or alcohol-free and the user has sensitive or dryness-related signals, that can strengthen the product-fit explanation. This should be framed as lower irritation potential, not as a medical guarantee.

When a formula includes barrier-supportive, soothing, or hydrating traits and the user has dryness, sensitivity, or dehydration-related signals, explanations should connect those traits to comfort and moisture support.

When a formula is described as lightweight, oil-free, or non-heavy and the user has oily or oily-acne signals, explanations should connect those traits to balanced hydration and reduced heaviness.

The product category also matters. A cleanser should be framed around cleansing style and skin feel, a moisturizer around hydration and comfort, an SPF around daily protection plus skin feel, and a serum around a more focused supporting role.
