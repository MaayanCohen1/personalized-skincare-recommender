# Safety Guidelines

Patch testing is a reasonable precaution before introducing a new skincare product. A small amount can be applied to a discreet area first to check whether the skin seems comfortable with the product before broader facial use.

When introducing products with more active or focused ingredients, gradual use may be more comfortable than starting too aggressively. This is especially relevant when the skin is already dry, sensitive, or visibly irritated.

Daily sunscreen use is an important part of routine support because UV exposure can worsen dryness, sensitivity, and uneven skin tone over time. Sunscreen explanations should frame SPF as a consistent daily-support step.

When multiple products in a routine include stronger active ingredients, it can be helpful to avoid overstating all of them at once. Explanations should stay conservative and should not imply that more actives always means a better routine.

Sensitive-feeling skin may be more reactive to fragranced or strongly drying products. When product metadata includes fragrance or drying alcohol and the user has sensitive or dryness-related signals, explanations should stay cautious and avoid “ideal for sensitive skin” wording unless clearly supported.

Skincare explanations should remain cosmetic and supportive in tone. They should avoid diagnosis, cure, treatment, prescription, or other medical framing.

A good explanation should describe why a product may suit the current routine and skin condition, not guarantee a specific outcome.
